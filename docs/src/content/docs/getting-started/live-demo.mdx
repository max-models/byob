This demo runs byob in your browser with [Pyodide](https://pyodide.org) (Python compiled to
WebAssembly): nothing is installed and nothing is sent to a server. A uniform plasma faces a
wall at $x = 0$; move the sliders to change the plasma and the wall.

The potential falls by $T_e/2$ across the presheath, which byob lets decay over 20 Debye
lengths, and then across the sheath down to the wall. A floating wall repels just enough
electrons to balance the ion flux: its sheath drop grows with $T_e$ and with the logarithm of
the ion mass (about $2.8\,T_e$ for hydrogen, $4.7\,T_e$ for argon), while its width stays a few
Debye lengths. A negatively biased wall widens the sheath following the Child-Langmuir law,
$s \propto \lambda_D\,\Delta\phi^{3/4}$; the density sets the Debye length and so the scale
of everything. In the sheath the electrons are repelled (Boltzmann) while the ions, accelerated
to the Bohm speed, conserve their flux, so the ion density stays above the electron density.

The same in a script, at particle positions:

```python
from simgeometry import Rectangle

from byob import Background, Probe, UniformGrid, Wall

grid = UniformGrid((0.0, 0.0), (0.05, 0.02), (51, 21))
probes = [
    Probe("density", (0.025, 0.01), 1e17),
    Probe("electron_temperature", (0.025, 0.01), 2.0),
    Probe("ion_temperature", (0.025, 0.01), 0.1),
]
wall = Wall(Rectangle((-0.01, 0.01), (0.02, 0.04)), "biased", -300.0)
background = Background.build(grid, probes, [wall])
state = background.evaluate([[1e-5, 0.01], [1e-3, 0.01]])  # potential, densities
```
